# MomentCircuit Simple TikTok Format System — 2026-09-27

## Principle

Do not over-design clipped videos.

The source footage is the content.
The headline earns the stop.
Everything else exists only when it helps comprehension.

TikTok's current official guidance favors TikTok-native, authentic creative over polished brand-style production, while retaining the fundamentals: vertical 9:16, high-resolution footage, safe-zone awareness, and an immediate hook.

## Visual constants

These stay consistent across MomentCircuit:

- canvas: 1080x1920 vertical;
- outer background: black;
- title always above the source video, never covering the important action;
- title palette: black / white / charcoal only by default;
- source video is visually dominant;
- no MomentCircuit logo bug on ordinary clips;
- no decorative footer;
- no progress bar;
- no fake chat/UI;
- no blurred filler background by default;
- no stock gameplay underneath unrelated clips;
- no watermark;
- Part label only when the post is genuinely part of a series.

Brand consistency comes from the composition, not from decorating every frame.

## Approved headline treatments

Only two primary treatments.

### A. Story / drama headline

Use for:
- streamer stories;
- celebrity/creator interactions;
- arguments;
- relationship stories;
- "what happened" clips;
- multi-part stories.

Look:
- black canvas;
- bold serif headline;
- black type inside simple white rounded rectangles;
- 1–4 lines;
- source video directly below;
- optional white "Part 1" rectangle at upper-left of video.

The headline should read like a strong tabloid/story headline, but remain factual.

Example:
"Coco Jones and Her Husband Can't Agree on How They Met"

### B. Fast / gaming headline

Use for:
- gaming;
- funny reactions;
- nostalgia;
- quick surprising moments;
- creator clips that do not need a story-style headline.

Look:
- black canvas;
- bold sans-serif headline;
- white type on charcoal/gray rounded background OR plain white text on black;
- 1–3 lines;
- optional single accent word only when it improves scanning;
- source video directly below.

Example:
"This Was The Craziest Warzone Moment Today"

## Color policy

Do not change the background color by campaign.

Default:
- background: black;
- headline boxes: white or charcoal;
- text: black or white.

Optional accent:
- one emphasized keyword can use a single cool accent such as cyan;
- never use more than one accent color in one headline;
- no campaign-branded rainbow styling unless the campaign explicitly requires it.

This keeps the account recognizable without making every video identical.

## Typography policy

Do **not** force one font across every topic.

Use only two approved families:
- Story/drama: bold serif.
- Gaming/fast: bold sans-serif.

Consistency comes from using the same two families repeatedly, not one universal font.

Avoid:
- novelty fonts;
- thin fonts;
- cursive;
- more than two typefaces in a post.

## Headline rules

Headline is one of the largest performance levers.

Default:
- 6–14 words;
- 1–4 lines;
- names/subjects included when useful for search;
- concrete, specific language;
- no vague "you won't believe this";
- no false stakes;
- no ending punctuation required unless it aids readability.

Good:
"He Celebrated Before Realizing What Chat Saw"

Weak:
"This Is Crazy 😱"

Search-aware:
Prefer "Coco Jones and Donovan..." over "They..."
Prefer "Warzone Operator Skin Toggle..." over "This New Feature..."

## Format selector

### FORMAT 1 — Story Headline + Clip
Default for narrative/people-driven material.

Input:
- headline;
- source clip.

Output:
- black canvas;
- white headline boxes / serif;
- video below.

### FORMAT 2 — Fast Hook + Clip
Default for gaming, nostalgia, quick reactions.

Input:
- concise hook;
- source clip.

Output:
- black canvas;
- charcoal/white title treatment / sans-serif;
- video below.

### FORMAT 3 — Part Series
Variant of Format 1 or 2.

Use only if:
- one clean video would be too long;
- there is a natural story break;
- each part has a real payoff;
- Part 2 is rendered before Part 1 is published.

Rules:
- two parts preferred;
- three parts rare;
- no empty cliffhanger;
- same headline treatment across parts;
- small "Part 1", "Part 2" box at upper-left of footage;
- Part 2 must still make sense if served first.

### FORMAT 4 — Caption-Assisted Clip
Same visual shell as Format 1/2.

Add subtitles only if:
- dialogue is difficult to understand;
- audio quality requires assistance;
- several speakers make comprehension difficult;
- campaign requires captions.

Subtitles:
- inside footage;
- white bold sans-serif with dark stroke/background;
- maximum two lines;
- lower safe zone;
- no karaoke rainbow words by default.

No subtitles when the raw audio is already clear.

### FORMAT 5 — Context / Explainer
For original 60–120 second MomentCircuit storytelling.

Keep the same visual simplicity:
- simple searchable title above;
- source clip / screenshots / context inside the central video region;
- narration if useful;
- occasional labels only when needed.

Do not turn this into a branded infographic template.

### FORMAT 6 — Comment / Search Response
Use after MomentCircuit has real comments/search behavior.

Replace the ordinary headline area with:
- a selected comment screenshot; or
- a short search/question prompt.

Then show the clip/context underneath.

TikTok's 2026 trend guidance specifically treats comments as a creative surface and emphasizes curiosity/search-led discovery.

### FORMAT 7 — Mini Compilation
Occasional.

Use for:
- "3 times...";
- multiple moments from the same creator/game/topic;
- roundup where all sources are authorized.

Rules:
- one headline;
- 2–4 related clips;
- hard clean cuts;
- no extra decorative layout.

Do not use compilation merely to make a weak clip longer.

## Format decision logic

1. Is the primary value a story about people / a sequence of events?
   -> Story Headline.

2. Is the value an immediate gaming/reaction/nostalgia moment?
   -> Fast Hook.

3. Does one video exceed a clean storytelling length and have a real natural break?
   -> 2-part Series.

4. Is raw dialogue hard to understand?
   -> add Caption-Assisted variant.
   Otherwise no subtitles.

5. Does the source moment need explanation/context to be meaningful or monetizably original?
   -> Context / Explainer.

6. Are viewers repeatedly asking the same question/comment?
   -> Comment / Search Response.

7. Are several authorized moments stronger together than separately?
   -> Mini Compilation.

Campaign rules override every decision above.

## Footage fitting

Vertical source:
- fill the video region naturally;
- do not crop faces/action.

Horizontal source:
- fit or crop intelligently depending on the subject;
- black letterboxing is acceptable and often native for theme pages;
- do not add random blurred copies of the footage behind it by default;
- do not stretch.

If cropping removes meaningful action, fit the original aspect ratio inside the black canvas.

## Safe zones

Important title/action must avoid platform UI.

Before posting:
- preview on the target platform;
- keep text away from right-side engagement controls;
- keep important information away from the bottom caption/navigation area;
- do not assume one safe-zone geometry fits every caption length/device.

TikTok and YouTube both provide safe-area guidance/tools because UI can cover edge content.

## Cover / profile grid

The first video frame should already make a usable cover when possible.

For headline-first formats:
- title visible immediately;
- title still readable in a smaller profile-grid preview;
- avoid the "Part" tag becoming the only readable cover text.

We can choose a separate cover where the platform supports it, but the video should not depend on that.

## Default usage mix

Approximately:
- 45% Story Headline;
- 35% Fast Hook;
- 10% Context/Explainer;
- 5% Part Series;
- 5% Comment/Compilation experiments.

These are experiment allocations, not algorithm facts.

Most output should remain Format 1 or Format 2.

## Quality rule

A strong video should look almost boring as a template:
- excellent headline;
- excellent moment;
- excellent crop;
- clean audio;
- simple frame.

If the design itself is what looks impressive, we probably over-designed it.
