### Video v1: important weaknesses

The cooking problem is commercially relevant, but the first image does not yet sell it physically: twelve pieces occupy only two rows while much of the tray remains empty. Falling extra food crosses a supporting label. A viewer can reasonably ask why the pieces cannot simply be spread out. The first seconds must make the constraint visible without claiming measured real pan capacity.

The actual-app segment consumes several seconds but crops out the six-serving counter and Adapt button. It then holds on a preceding sauce note and a cut-off broil instruction, never showing the key two-sheet guidance. That is the central product proof, not a decorative screen-recording detail. The real chosen inputs and actual changed method must be visible, with historical-result disclosure, before the illustrated resolution can credibly be attributed to RecipeFix.

The transition into two trays currently replaces/fades the original arrangement rather than continuously moving the same eighteen objects. This weakens the cause-and-consequence story. The trays then take turns on a rack, which is the right payoff mechanism. Root has identified the continuity and crop problems and is revising them; proposed fixes are not counted as inspected improvements yet.

The ending returns to a static Original photo/URL card for roughly four seconds after the practical answer is understood. It risks repeating the old end-card tax. A shorter invitation with a direct, feasible recipe destination would use this time better. The video is product-connected now, but its long UI hold plus final card leave too little evolving action in the first export.
