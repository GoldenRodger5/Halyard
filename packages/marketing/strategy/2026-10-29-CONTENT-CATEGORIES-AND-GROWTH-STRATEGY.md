# RecipeFix — Content Categories and Growth Strategy
**Decision date:** October 29, 2026
**Status:** founder product direction; content-strategy canon, not permission to publish, buy subscriptions or change live accounts.
**Read with:** the Founder Operating Model (Oct 28), Social Studio planning index, PR #104 founder creative rejection and PR #105 product/growth critique.

## North star: interesting food brand, real RecipeFix business

RecipeFix is the flagship. The purpose of marketing is not simply impressive videos, follower counts or winning an animation competition. Our outcome is a growing audience of relevant cooks who discover RecipeFix, complete a *useful first adaptation*, save/cook/plan and return, and eventually pay for Plus.

The Oct 28 founder audit verified one genuine active monthly Stripe payer and a paid $3.99 renewal. An Apple annual entitlement exists but its current net proceeds were not verified. These facts are a modest business baseline, not complete lifetime revenue. We must urgently learn whether acquisition, first value, weekly habit or Plus value is the main bottleneck. Never confuse social engagement with revenue.

**Business + creative both matter.** The social brand should be joyful, curious, entertaining, educational, occasionally weird and visually memorable. But a meaningful share of posts MUST make the cooking need and RecipeFix benefit legible. Good content can be unapologetically fun without an app pitch; the *portfolio* must still create routes to try the app.

The previous 70%-games-first social launch strategy is **SUPERSEDED as the default**. PR #104 app films were rejected as boring slide decks. PR #105 yolk-game and popcorn-science exports are potentially useful content R&D, not evidence that pure entertainment earns customers and not approved publishing inventory. No automatic new character universe.

## Proposed pilot mix — NOT a rigid forever quota

For a first 14-day, founder-approved content experiment, start at approximately:
- **50% — RecipeFix product and recipe problem-solving:** seven of fourteen primary ideas. Objective: qualified intent → first successful adaptation → saved/cooked result.
- **30% — useful, problem-adjacent food content:** four of fourteen ideas. Objective: attract cooks with actual ingredient, diet, headcount, nutrition and technique questions; earn trust; sometimes bridge to app naturally.
- **20% — entertaining food community:** three of fourteen ideas. Objective: follower engagement, comments, saves, shares, personality, audience questions. No forced product call-to-action.

These are editorial hypotheses; the founder can change ratios after genuine audience/activation evidence. More than one output may be made from an idea, but only where it makes sense for the platform. Do not fill a quota with low-quality posts. Start with 3–4 excellent live-ready concepts if 14 are not ready.

## Category taxonomy (15 distinct content lanes)

The category describes the *viewer experience and business job*; the format is chosen later. Do not turn each category into one repetitive visual template.

| Code | Category | Why a viewer cares / creative example | Business role / natural bridge |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | **RecipeFix introduction / the promise** | A short memorable story: “That recipe you love, now for the way you eat.” Authentic visual problem and transformation, not app-logo slideshow. | Explain what the app actually does; clearly invite a first try. |
| P02 | **Recipes to adapt / recipe rescue** | A genuinely appetizing, attributed recipe whose existing ingredients conflict with a person's restriction, servings or goal; meaningful transformation and changed technique. | Highest-intent format: user brings own recipe to Adapt. |
| P03 | **Feature magic / real app walkthrough** | One actual useful action per episode: Adapt, calories/protein, Cook Mode, grocery, saved variations, serving changes. Fast, accurate screen-capture and relevant outcome. | Make a real feature understandable and worth using. |
| P04 | **Nutrition targets and honest tradeoffs** | “Can this become high-protein without becoming dry?” Actual RecipeFix output shows what changed, what missed and why. No fake food proof. | Appeal to target-driven cooks; distinguish responsible adaptations from numerical gimmicks. |
| P05 | **Cooking the week with RecipeFix** | Recipe → save → plan → grocery → Cook, shown through a real coherent result, not a list of features. | Give users a repeatable reason to return and consider Plus. |
| P06 | **Ingredient swaps / original versus adapted** | Show ingredient roles, texture, quantity and a real related step change, with a useful practical takeaway. | Demonstrate differentiation from a simple swap chart. |
| E01 | **Teach me something / cooking technique** | Sauce consistency, heat, ingredient purpose, prep or kitchen fixes with truthful visual action. | Build trust among cooks; app tie-in optional if relevant. |
| E02 | **Did you know? / surprising food science** | One sourced discovery with a visible cause and meaningful payoff (not five vague warnings). | Curiosity and useful knowledge; attract people who cook. |
| E03 | **Food history, origin and culture** | Short well-sourced stories and debates, clear cultural context and a satisfying answer or uncertainty. | Discovery and brand affinity; product connection only when natural. |
| E04 | **Kitchen math, scaling and measurement** | Visual comparison of serving quantities, cups versus grams, batch sizes, conversions. Cite evidence; never fabricate readings. | Especially strong fit for real RecipeFix scaling intent and high-intent web traffic. |
| E05 | **Recipes, seasonal inspiration and food ideas** | Beautiful, actually sourced recipe spotlights, seasonal menus and cooking inspiration. | Help viewers find a recipe worth saving or adapting. |
| C01 | **Food games / interactive challenges** | Follow the Yolk, ingredient guessing, recipe roulette, spot the difference, mini interactive challenges. A real payoff, not engagement bait. | Awareness and community; only occasional promotion. |
| C02 | **Food trivia, quizzes, this-or-that** | Fun sourced questions, option reveals, graphical carousel/short, platform polls where supported. | Replies, saves, recurring series, relevant audience learning. |
| C03 | **Humor / relatable cooking culture** | A clever kitchen mishap, expectation-versus-reality or absurd ingredient substitution handled without fake real-world evidence. | Brand personality and shares; no mandatory app CTA. |
| C04 | **Community questions, replies and participation** | Answer actual user comments, let the audience pick an adaptation, clarify a follower question. Only quote/feature people with consent. | Conversation → product insight → a reason to return. |

Seasonal events are **cross-cutting angles**, not a reason to build a new content engine. Series names may recur; their ideas, visual treatment and editorial goals should vary. Diverse communities/cuisines should be represented respectfully without making sweeping diet, religious, allergen or nutrition claims.

Use verified platform constraints rather than inventing that an API supports carousels, Stories, native polls, auto-replies or unsupported scheduling. A platform may have zero variant for a given idea. YouTube Shorts remains video-only, not a carousel destination. Exact post format and source proof belong in an approval manifest.

## Conversion bridge, not a relentless advertisement

A post must have a *reason* to exist and a *next action appropriate to it*.

- **Product/problem post:** explicit invitation to try the real recipe/adaptation, with verified CTA and tagged destination where supported.
- **Practical food post:** give the useful tip first, optionally invite people to see how a recipe is adapted, saveable carousel/related resource in profile.
- **Fun community post:** satisfying ending and real conversation prompt; no unnatural “download now” sales slab.
- **Profile and series:** explain quickly why RecipeFix exists, show current working product, clear trustworthy path to Adapt. Have real useful content pinned only after founder approval and platform compatibility.

Do not put an unclickable link in every post just to tick a conversion box. Choose feasible bio/profile links, pinned comment only where actually supported, and correctly tagged landing destinations. An interesting game may increase followers but no immediate app clicks; evaluate *portfolio* results rather than requiring every creative to sell.

**Do not use clickbait** (“number 4 will shock you”), false diet certification, invented app adaptation numbers, fabricated cooking footage, synthetic testimonials or unverifiable food history.

## Commercial measurement and learning

Track per idea, platform and creative variant:
1. **Attention:** reached people, 2s/6s holds where available, Shorts chose-to-view/average percent viewed, shares/saves/comments. Different platform definitions are not interchangeable.
2. **Qualified interest:** meaningful profile visits, tagged landing sessions and comments expressing a genuine recipe problem.
3. **First product value:** first successfully completed real adaptation, save/Cook/Plan continuation where event coverage permits.
4. **Return and monetization:** actual repeat useful actions, verified Plus upgrade and provider-backed subscription/renewal facts. Never infer MRR from a success-page event or manual grant.
5. **Efficiency:** founder time per 14-day batch, marginal render/media expense, accepted creatives per session; avoid endless polishing of unpublished posts.

Use existing RecipeFix analytics/PostHog/UTM and authoritative Stripe/Apple records. Exclude test/founder/bot traffic where possible. Missing attribution is an unknown, not a zero-conversion fact. We have not run a real new campaign or proved conversion uplift; all suggested category ratios are hypotheses.

## Founder-approved two-week working rhythm

1. **Research brief:** source-linked food trends, creator styles, comments/owned audience needs, key recipe search terms and measured past post performance; identify high-intent problems.
2. **Editorial collaboration:** founder spends one or two days selecting 14 ideas maximum, hooks, mood, genre, platforms, and approving actual media; reject boring concepts before a full render.
3. **Creative production:** reuse Halyard, Remotion/Playwright/Sharp and optional tested new tools; original art direction per genre; real UI/recipe truth; inspect full output visually and audio where tools permit.
4. **Approve exact variants:** file hash, caption, rights, AI disclosure, destination account, scheduled local time. Never reuse rejected creative. No automatic auto-approval after a month.
5. **Automated operations:** approved schedule and publishing per verified platform API, idempotency/status; collect actual analytics and ongoing research and founder-review-only engagement drafts.
6. **Next session:** compare meaningful product actions and audience questions, adjust category weight and format, retire weak series, protect successful learnings.

Budget: as close to free as practical, ideally ~$20/month recurring. Blotato deserves capability verification against Buffer and native alternatives, not blind replacement. Founder approval before subscriptions, paid AI video, outbound community activity or public posting.
