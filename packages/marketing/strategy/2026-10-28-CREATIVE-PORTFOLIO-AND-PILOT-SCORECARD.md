# RecipeFix Social Studio — Creative Portfolio, Pilots, Experiments and 14-Day Rhythm
2026-10-28 | Editorial design and experiment suggestions, NOT approved posts or facts ready for publication.

## What our content should feel like
RecipeFix has permission to be a lively FOOD brand, not merely a troubleshooting app, allergies warning page or serious instructional feed. Some days: clever cooking games, funny posts and surprising food history. Other days: real RecipeFix adaptation, genuine nutrition and method explanations, appetizing recipe ideas, product discovery and helpful tips. Use diverse visual language: magazine-style bright photography/graphics, energetic product cinema, experimental animation, playful game design and occasional cinematic AI illustrative motion. Brand coherence comes from distinctive quality and subtle color/typography identity; not identical five-point dark cards, constant white captions or mandatory synthetic narration.

### The content ladder (editorial hypothesis)
| Level | Purpose | Examples | Suggested primary forms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discovery | Earn attention from people who have never heard of us | Food trivia, science experiment, unlikely ingredient fact, food history, cooking humor | TikTok short, IG Reel, Shorts, X image/poll |
| Community | Give them a reason to interact, follow and return | Pick a swap, recipe roulette, trivia showdown, ingredient guessing challenge, follower question | IG carousel/Story, X poll, TikTok photo game, replies |
| Product magic | Show why RecipeFix is different | Adapt changes an ingredient AND its cooking method; nutritional tradeoff; intelligent Cook serving changes | Real UI recording + motion, IG original/adapted carousel, X screenshot |
| Activation | Turn interest into useful first recipe | Link-to-Adapt real flow, free first try, Cook and Plan demonstration, saved result | Detailed product short, swipe guide, useful pinned profile link |
| Retention | Reinforce how to use it weekly | Meal planning, grocery→kitchen flow, saved versions/notes, weekly cooking routine | Feature tip, carousel, timely user Q&A |

Do not make every social post a hard sell. Promotion must be accurate, valuable and approachable. Where a recipe outcome is unverified physically, show only the actual app's source/changed instructions, not fabricated plated results.

## Twelve creative territories — not twelve templates
1. **RecipeFix in action:** real source link → Read → Make it fit → meaningful ingredient + method change, elegantly paced, real UI.
2. **The ingredient courtroom:** flour, sugar, coconut milk, peanut butter, eggs etc. argue for their cooking jobs using controlled vector animation—not fictional dietary claims.
3. **Recipe Rescue:** what changed to save a tricky diet/nutrition request; honestly show unmet targets if any.
4. **A tiny food documentary:** short historically sourced story with illustrated timeline, interesting detail and payoff.
5. **Pick your plate / recipe roulette:** playful choose-one food/cooking challenge; visually different platform polls/quiz posts.
6. **Baking math reveal:** compare verified official weight-chart values via graphical numeric reveal. Real scale footage only if actually captured; never invent readings.
7. **What is in my kitchen?:** animated Pantry-to-Grocery flow, demonstrate actual RecipeFix features without busy UI screen grabs.
8. **The wild origin:** disputed food origins treated carefully as interesting debates, not made-up definitive historical claims.
9. **Expectation vs reality:** funny low-stakes kitchen experiences with original creative assets/graphics.
10. **One swap, five consequences?** Narrow to one meaningful recipe and show texture/method tradeoff instead of five unrelated warnings.
11. **A 20-second product feature:** actual Cook Mode timer, original/adapted toggle, nutrition by ingredient or shopping list, authentic result.
12. **Audience cooks with us:** invite a real question about a recipe; research agent turns recurring *approved* questions into next week's original post.

### Six more expansion candidates
- Seasonal “why this ingredient appears now” food history.
- This-or-that ingredients with a real sensory/cooking-function question.
- Useful packaged-food label read, using actual licensed/verifiable label evidence if available.
- International substitution traditions (respect culture and cite appropriate sources).
- What a recipe instruction actually means, animated but grounded.
- One flavor chemistry comparison with safe accurate explanation.

## Four premium pilots — concrete storyboard-level experiments

### Pilot P1: Product Cinema — a genuine Adapt transformation
Audience: cooks with a restriction or goal who doubt simple substitutions are enough.
Visual concept: fast beautiful original recipe image (rights validated) → clean zoom onto real RecipeFix link input → click/read → actual “Make it fit” request with a safely seeded user → ingredients change → associated METHOD line changes → final recipe preview/what was achieved (only measured values). Use a crisp full-frame edit with app UI cropping, kinetic annotations that highlight real changes, no robotic tutorial narration. Closing payoff: “It changed HOW you cook it, too.” (only if true for this actual example). Optional quiet music and mouse/keyboard sounds.
Need: safe QA credentials/test recipe, real Playwright recording, verified output, hashed provenance, licensable image, source/rights log. No customer data. Versions: 14–24s punchy TikTok; 20–35s self-contained Shorts edit; 5–6-panel IG carousel original→swap→method→takeaway; X actual screenshot with useful copy. Control = current Halyard Remotion; challenger = HyperFrames same script and assets.

### Pilot P2: Food game — same cup? Different mass!
Audience: food/cooking curiosity, casual bakers.
Visual concept: two illustrated cups → choose flour or sugar → digital illustrated numbers from a verified published flour/sugar reference chart → satisfying reveal, one practical baking explanation. No fake “live scale recording.” Bright palette and playful motion, textured background or polished illustrated glass/food assets. Source in caption, not unreadable font. Ending invitation “Which one fooled you?” Versions: TikTok animated game, IG answer-on-last-slide carousel, X poll with later source-backed follow-up, Shorts complete 20–30s explainer if it warrants one.
Tool bakeoff: existing Remotion vs Motion Canvas *only after* a credible game timeline is designed, and Sharp/Satori for cards.

### Pilot P3: Beautiful editorial carousel — one interesting food origin
Audience: history/trivia/culture curious.
Visual: sophisticated cover with a surprising but verifiable question; 5–7 slides with one distinct piece of visual information per frame; beautiful licensed food/ingredient imagery, simple map/timeline/era cues, ending provides real answer or explicitly unresolved historical uncertainty. Native IG carousel and X concise one-post/thread variant, maybe 12s animated story for TikTok/Shorts only if visuals meaningfully move. Satori + Sharp comparison; avoid repeating darkened stock.
Needs reputable primary/secondary historical sources, review for overconfident origin claims and rights.

### Pilot P4: Imaginative AI scene — ingredient motion or animated concept
Audience: visual delight; not meant to prove real food was cooked.
Visual: ingredient shapes swirl/assemble into an impossible but attractive animated metaphor, then cut to factual app/UI or a punchline/quiz. Limit AI generation to 2–5s; generate only with provider authorization and cost cap. Compare local code animation to LTX 2.3 Fast 720x1280 (~$0.15 for 5s) and optionally Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro (~$0.35 for 5s). No AI package labels, scale readings, physical recipe success, or fake customer hands/creator.
Review real exported clip for coherent object identity, seamless action, typography constraints, right aspect, non-uncanny food, IP/rights, correct disclosure. Pick the better affordable clip; keep no-AI choice if strongest.

## Creative evidence and measurement rubric (pilot review, 0–2 each, not a single blind gate)
- First-second visual promise, independent of a large generic title.
- Visual action evolves; movement changes story or viewer understanding.
- Useful or emotionally satisfying payoff.
- Creative originality, art direction and attractive food/app presentation.
- Content/channel fit and conversion bridge appropriate to this post.
- Caption/typography readability at 360px and 390px; not tiny data everywhere.
- Sound choice/pacing, both muted and on, and no robotic voice if used.
- Factual and media provenance, platform disclosure, privacy and brand safety.
- Reliable source/method/ingredient fidelity for real app demos.
- Actual production cost, source editability and repeatability.

**Hard veto regardless of high score:** fabricated outcome/measurement, wrong brand account, missing usage rights, misleading health/diet safety, badly broken visual/audio, hidden incomplete UI interaction, owner-rejected creative. Peer reviewers report risks, not final auto-approval.

### QA and learning measures, once published
- Stop/hold: TikTok 2s/6s, YouTube chose-to-view and avg % viewed, retention by platform; not one common “views” definition.
- Engagement: eligible saves/shares/replies, question themes, profile visits/follower growth.
- Real product: UTMs, successful first adaptations, saves/returns, verified signups and subscriptions, with QA/crawler exclusions where available and *only valid attribution*.
- Production efficiency: cost per accepted creative and time to assemble the next approved 14-day slate, not only total generated post count.
- Research: source-linked idea freshness and whether it informs successful content, not number of scraped comments.
- No promised MRR or virality. Small-sample recommendations stay provisional.
