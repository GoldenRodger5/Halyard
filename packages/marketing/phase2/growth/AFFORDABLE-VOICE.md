## Where the quality improvement should come from

1. Lock the actual visual sequence first. Write conversational words for those actions rather than narrating a slide deck. Keep useful pauses and let visual action carry part of the story.
2. Cast a grounded, non-announcer voice. Preserve the Roger rejection. Compare a small number of full-script takes in the real edit, not a library of isolated auditions. A designed voice may reduce the same-stock-voice feel, but [ElevenLabs explicitly says Voice Design quality varies](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/voices/voice-design); it is a future bounded test, not a guarantee.
3. Use a natural pace and phrase-level emphasis. Avoid rigid sentence-by-sentence splicing when it breaks connected delivery; any limited line repair should preserve the full performance. Official [TTS guidance](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices) emphasizes voice choice, speech patterns and punctuation. A larger plan does not replace those choices.
4. Mix voice against real purposeful effects/music, keep intelligibility, and compare sound-on playback at a consistent listening level. Signal/peak/alignment checks remain objective; the founder's actual listening decides warmth, naturalness and annoyance. No agent here has directly heard a new take.

Next action is visual keep/revise/stop selection. The voice experiment remains **held**, not queued or automatically triggered when rendering ends.
