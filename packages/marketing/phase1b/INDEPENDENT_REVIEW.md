### Follow the yolk — final critique

The opening offers an understandable task immediately. The yolk settles under the middle shell, the same shell follows readable crossings, and the concealment now works before the tracking action begins. The short numbered guess beat is a real question; generic “still with it” / “wait for it” prompts are gone. The escaped empty shell receives a visibly surprised reaction when the chopsticks intervene.

The final callback is a meaningful improvement over v3's bounce/reset. First the actual middle answer is revealed fairly. The yolk then rolls away, the same chopsticks arrive too late, and “OH.” plus the middle shell's expression supplies a second comic reward. The audience receives an ending beyond having tracked the easiest shell. This is the first of these experiments I would prioritize for founder viewing as a possible recurring character/game format.

Remaining limits are substantial taste questions: the game is easy; smiling food faces and a fixed stage are familiar animation devices; much of the world remains unchanged around the action. It is charming and legible, but those qualities do not establish a distinctive flagship identity or broad social appeal. The final empty-air chopstick gesture is restrained rather than a large physical gag. It is not a seamless loop and no seamless-loop claim should be made. I found no remaining concealment, title-collision or arbitrary-answer defect requiring another render.

### The little explosion — final critique

The 13.5-second revision gets to the interior earlier and reduces the long initial/final holds. Steam particles, pressure waves and hull strain give the middle a developing state. Most importantly, rupture now visibly separates two hull pieces and the interior perimeter expands into the payoff. The former replacement-image feel is materially reduced. Removing eye-like amber fragments and the all-over radial flower makes the final shape less accidentally face-like; the irregular folded body, cavity and retained hull make it more specific to popcorn.

It remains a restrained science illustration. The opening/cutaway is mainly a graphic transition, not an immersive camera flight inside a kernel. The droplet icon, concentric pressure waves, arrows and explanatory captions still carry several seconds of the story. The reveal is expected for anyone who recognizes popcorn, so it has less surprise and personality than the game. The smooth vector folds remain stylized, with limited material texture; do not present this as photoreal food cinema or simulated microscopy. I would consider it for a useful educational series, but not use its export success to assert the founder's “visually exceptional” ambition has been met. No further small typography/texture pass is requested by this review.

## Rejected-film diagnosis

The authoritative [founder rejection on Halyard PR104](https://github.com/GoldenRodger5/Halyard/pull/104#issuecomment-6500212414) supersedes the earlier B-for-clarity/A-for-warmth preference and withdrawn hybrid proposal. A4 and B2 remain rejected research, not inventory. No hybrid or reuse of their23-second storyboard is recommended.

The central weakness is a story with no developing question. The opening supplies the whole proposition—cream changed, method changed—then successive source crops validate it. Approximately3–18seconds show configuration, ingredient and method evidence; the viewer mainly reads and waits for the next card. Perspective, zooms and wipes move the presentation rather than change the situation. The five-second ending arrives after the useful discovery is exhausted. Clear evidence is valuable, but it does not create a reason to keep watching. A warmer palette or shorter CTA cannot cure this premise.

The earlier independent review was too permissive about a format-level weakness. It correctly named the deck rhythm but treated it as founder taste after engineering defects were fixed. For Phase1B, the developing visual event and payoff must be judged before production, not relegated to a caveat after export.

## Three-concept gate before production

**A — Save the yolk: develop.** An actual tracking question and character behavior give this a potential reason to watch. Establish the yolk under one identifiable shell, keep2–3 crossings fair, let an empty shell misbehave, then resolve the same question visibly. The viewer must be able to track or knowingly lose the shell; an arbitrary reveal would invalidate the game. A brief deliberate guess pause is acceptable. Decorative whites ribboning into a bowl would be a disconnected payoff; that element was removed. The reset should arise from the yolk's action. Risk: an ordinary shell shuffle with cute faces is still generic. The comic interruption and satisfying physical behavior must carry its identity.

**B — The tiny pressure cooker: develop.** This is genuinely different from A: curiosity about what happens inside a recognizable kernel, escalating strain, then a single inside-out popcorn reveal. Follow one causal path from water/steam through pressure to rupture and expansion. Keep the kernel recognizable; a glowing tunnel/particle montage would be interchangeable stock spectacle. The pressure-cooker phrase is an analogy, not a literal cutaway. Scientific details need primary factual support and an explicit illustrative treatment. Risk: a pretty cross-section that merely holds while labels explain it would recreate the rejected format.

**C — The guest list broke the recipe: kill before production.** Chairs, cream cart, coconut replacement, whisk bridge, separated stream and proof inset compete to explain one result. Understanding the metaphor would be work; the same old cream story risks returning to the rejected case study. It could also suggest the app physically repairs/cooks sauce. A product territory remains worth exploring later, but this treatment is not among the strongest two.

The producer accepted this gate: A+B selected, C killed before full production. Selected A+B offer genuinely different experiences. This is permission to test the concepts, not creative acceptance of unrendered work.

### Egg v1

The viewer has a real task, the shell trajectories visibly cross, and the little yolk/terracotta/cobalt world is more distinctive than a product deck. The answer follows a consistent shell path, middle→left→right→middle, rather than being chosen arbitrarily. The motion has an actual subject and consequence. This is a stronger creative premise than the old case study.

The v1 yolk is visibly protruding beneath the covered shell through much of the game. That destroys the intended tracking challenge. Initial raised shells collide with the title. Root independently caught both and has code changes pending; these are still defects of the actual v1 until a new encoded export is inspected.

Beyond those fixes, the caption sequence “STILL WITH IT?” and “WAIT FOR IT” feels like generic retention prompting. One short genuine guess pause is enough. The empty shell's attempted escape is the most promising character beat, but the arriving chopsticks are unexplained and the shells keep nearly identical contented expressions. A clear startled reaction and consequence would add more than another object or caption. The tracking action is a reason to watch; the characters must make it memorable.

The ending lowers all shells over the yolk, whereas the opening has the yolk arrive from the left. That is a reset but not a seamless loop; do not claim a seamless loop. A brief reveal-and-reset is preferable to extending the answer just to fill runtime. The final owner should assess whether the game is pleasantly fair or merely easy after the protrusion fix. No public engagement claim is established.

### Popcorn v1

The kernel silhouette and restrained copper/ink world are coherent. Water→steam→pressure→rupture→expanded starch is a useful causal story, unlike the old product-proof sequence. However, the actual first draft still behaves like illustrated science cards. The exterior holds, the cutaway holds with a droplet, the same cutaway holds with particles/arrows, a crack appears, then a popcorn body replaces it. Captions do much of the developing work. Approximately3–10seconds remain too similar, and the final beauty state lasts about4seconds. I would not keep watching this version on visual curiosity alone.

At exact11.3seconds, newly drawn fluff grows over a fading intact cross-section; by11.7seconds the original interior is gone. The reveal therefore reads as replacing one image with another, rather than the interior bursting through its hull. This is the highest-impact action change: separate hull halves visibly and let identifiable interior material expand outward through the opening. A shorter timeline or more camera drift alone would not solve it. The resulting round cloud lobes also resemble cauliflower; an irregular folded/pitted popcorn form and fragments of retained hull would make the payoff specific. This is not a request for endless surface polish—the transformation is the concept's reward.

Root's requested camera entry, shortened middle/ending and visible pressure consequences are appropriate. The revised export must show that these changes create a developing event; the proposed changes themselves are not evidence that the film works.
