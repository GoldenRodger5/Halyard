# Phase 1B — concept gate, October 29, 2026

PR104 A4/B2 are rejected by the founder, including the withdrawn hybrid. Their central verbs were display, read and zoom. They exhausted the proposition before the middle and made viewers wait through proof and a five-second CTA. Preserve them as technical R&D, never selected inventory.

## A — Follow the yolk (selected)

Reason to watch: a fair visual tracking game with a mischievous interruption, resolved on screen. No prior app knowledge or factual homework.

Storyboard: 0–2s a glossy yolk bounces into the middle of three sculptural eggshells; 2–6s two readable crossing exchanges; 6–7s an empty shell tries escaping and is nudged back; 7–9s a last exchange; 9–10s a short guess breath; 10–12s empty shells lift, the tracked shell reveals the yolk; 12–15s the yolk celebrates, catches its shell and resets. No arbitrary answer, comment bait, recipe claim or sales slab.

Medium: original layered vector illustration with gradients, soft shadows, shell speckles and character animation. Cobalt kitchen stage, warm yolk, oversized restrained type. Frame-driven paths, squash/stretch, anticipation and impacts. Locally authored percussion follows crossings, pauses before the reveal, then resolves.

Risk: a familiar shell game can still be ordinary. The test is readable movement, comic character and a satisfying reveal, rather than more effects. Do not add a second unrelated fact or a decorative egg-white payoff.

## B — The little explosion (selected)

Reason to watch: discover an ordinary snack from an impossible intimate viewpoint, with a single earned physical transformation.

Storyboard: recognizable kernel already strains in the first second; follow water into a cutaway; heat and trapped moisture visibly drive pressure; shell stretches/cracks; one decisive rupture expands starch into recognizable asymmetric popcorn; a quiet final beauty beat resolves the tension. About 17s, no fixed 23-second structure.

Medium: original macro illustration, camera choreography, masks, deterministic particles, warm copper/ink atmosphere. Few short explanatory labels tied to visible action. Sound rises with pressure, cuts before rupture and decays after it. Illustrative model, not lab footage or an exact simulation. Sources: ACS and Popcorn Board; no numeric temperature/pressure claim needed.

Risk: generic glowing orb/tunnel visuals would fail. Maintain the kernel identity and causal connection through the cutaway and show a recognizable snack at the end.

## C — The guest list broke the recipe (killed before full production)

Proposed storyboard: two chairs join a four-place table; a cream cart exits and coconut enters; a whisk bridge repairs the separated stream; an actual recorded RecipeFix method appears as proof. Uses the genuine four-to-six dairy-free adaptation, without claiming a physically cooked result.

Independent gate: too many metaphors (chairs/cart/bridge/stream/proof), repeats the old cream story and risks implying the app cooks or repairs food. A proof inset would return to the rejected presentation. Not worth a third full render now. Product stories need a simpler dramatic premise in a later phase.

## Independent pre-production decision

The reviewer selected A+B and rejected C before production. Key instruction: core verbs must be visual events. A must remain fair and trackable; B must keep a recognizable kernel and one causal inside-out payoff. Reviewer's judgment is advisory; founder retains creative veto. No scores or renderer changes can confer publication approval.

## Technology choice after story selection

Existing Remotion can choreograph custom SVG geometry, gradients, masks, paths and deterministic particles without a second engine. HyperFrames already proved capable in Phase1A but did not solve the concept. Motion Canvas remains useful for complex vector teaching timelines; no new renderer is required for these scenes. Satori targets static graphics rather than this action. No libraries, GPU models, subscriptions or paid generation are installed/called for this phase.
