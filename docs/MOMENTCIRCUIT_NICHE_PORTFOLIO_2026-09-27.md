# MomentCircuit Niche Portfolio — 2026-09-27

## Decision

MomentCircuit is not a generic clipping page.

It is a digital-culture entertainment brand built around:
- gaming;
- streamers;
- creators/influencers;
- celebrity/music/pop-culture moments;
- culturally relevant entertainment stories.

The account should feel coherent enough that a viewer who likes one post is likely to care about the next.

## Research basis

### Audience behavior

TikTok's 2026 trend forecast emphasizes "Reali-Tea": unfiltered stories, behind-the-scenes moments, community, honesty, and curiosity-led discovery.

YouTube's September 2026 Culture & Trends research reports:
- YouTube creators are perceived as widely known by 77% of surveyed respondents;
- memes: 78%;
- Hollywood celebrities: 79%;
- 71% have watched videos unpacking stories inside a fandom/community;
- 69% have watched content from a niche community;
- 73% say YouTube is the best place to deeply explore personal interests.

This favors creator lore, streamer moments, gaming culture, fandom explainers, and culturally relevant celebrity/pop-culture storytelling.

YouTube Shorts average more than 200B daily views, and YouTube Live reported more than 600M logged-in viewers watching live content/archive per day in August 2026. Live-stream culture is therefore a large source pool, not a fringe niche.

### Paid campaign market

Content Rewards currently demonstrates meaningful paid demand in:
- gaming;
- streamers;
- sports/card breaks;
- creator/celebrity/music;
- podcasts/talk shows;
- licensed entertainment/show footage;
- software/AI;
- consumer products.

Examples observed:
- Call of Duty campaign: tens of thousands of dollars spent, $1.75 CPM in recent crawl;
- Fanatics/eBay live card-break campaigns: commonly $2–$3 CPM;
- CoCo Jones: $2 CPM on TikTok/IG/YouTube in current launch brief;
- Backyard Breaks: $2 CPM, 26.6M approved views, top clippers in the multi-thousands;
- Marcus Mumford: $2 TikTok CPM with top clippers in the hundreds;
- officially supplied show clips have also run around $2 CPM.

Paid inventory changes rapidly; campaign presence is evidence of demand, not guaranteed future supply.

## Main-account pillars

### Tier A — Core

#### 1. Gaming moments
**Priority: highest**

Includes:
- game launches;
- streamer gameplay reactions;
- insane wins/fails;
- new-feature moments;
- gaming controversy explained factually;
- tournament/event reactions;
- game lore when tied to a current moment.

Why:
- large campaign budgets exist;
- gaming naturally produces visually immediate moments;
- gaming and livestream culture overlap heavily;
- strong sponsor/affiliate upside later (games, gear, accessories, events);
- easy to source from authorized campaigns/creators.

Formats:
- "this changed the whole match";
- "he realized one second too late";
- "why everyone is talking about this update";
- "the moment chat knew it was over".

#### 2. Streamer / creator moments
**Priority: highest**

Includes:
- Twitch/Kick/YouTube creator moments;
- funny reactions;
- interactions;
- arguments without rage-bait distortion;
- wholesome/awkward moments;
- creator-event moments;
- behind-the-scenes clips;
- "what happened next?" context.

Why:
- TikTok 2026 trend research favors unfiltered/BTS/authentic moments;
- live content is massive on YouTube;
- creator fandom is now mainstream culture;
- paid clipping supply already exists.

This should be the identity anchor alongside gaming.

#### 3. Creator / internet-culture lore
**Priority: highest owned format**

This is the transformation layer, not just clipping.

Examples:
- "why chat completely lost it here";
- "what happened before this viral clip";
- "the 30 seconds that started this whole feud";
- "why this moment mattered";
- "what everyone missed in this clip".

Why:
- 71% of YouTube's surveyed audience has watched fandom/community story-unpacking content;
- creates original narration/context;
- improves native monetization durability;
- lets one source moment produce both a 20–40s growth cut and a 60–120s story cut.

This is the single most important owned-content format for MomentCircuit.

### Tier A- — Expansion

#### 4. Celebrity / music / pop-culture moments

Includes:
- artist interviews;
- celebrity interactions;
- fashion/red-carpet/social moments;
- official behind-the-scenes material;
- release/career moments;
- relationships/life stories only where source rights and facts are clean.

Why:
- fandom behavior is strong and participatory;
- current clipping campaigns exist for artists/celebrities;
- fits MomentCircuit's "digital culture moment" promise.

Do not become a gossip/rumor page.
Unresolved allegations are attributed or excluded.

#### 5. Officially licensed reality/show/entertainment moments

Use only:
- campaign-supplied show footage;
- explicit commercial clipping permission;
- genuinely transformative commentary/review excerpts where rights review permits.

Why:
- reality/drama naturally has hooks, tension, romance and payoff;
- official campaigns prove that rights-cleared show clipping inventory exists;
- culturally adjacent to creator/pop-culture content.

Raw ripped TV scenes are excluded.

### Tier B — Selective

#### 6. High-energy podcast / talk-show moments

Only publish if the moment has:
- visual/emotional action;
- a sharp story;
- surprising opinion/reveal;
- cultural relevance;
- creator/celebrity crossover.

Avoid:
- generic talking-head motivation;
- random business advice;
- slow informational clips.

Paid podcast campaigns exist, but generic podcast clips can weaken the entertainment identity.

#### 7. Sports spectacle / card-break reactions

Economically attractive:
- current/recent Fanatics, eBay Live and Backyard Breaks campaigns have paid roughly $2–$3 CPM;
- top clippers in card-break campaigns have reached thousands of dollars.

However audience overlap with streamer/gaming MomentCircuit is imperfect.

Rule:
- MomentCircuit may test rare crossover moments with strong internet-culture appeal;
- do not turn the main page into a sports-card page.

If repeated sports/card campaign supply remains strong, launch a separate sports-spectacle/collectibles brand after MomentCircuit proves the operating model.

## Separate-account niches

These may be valuable, but should not share MomentCircuit.

### A. Bodycam / real-incident stories
Strength:
- enormous view potential;
- strong "what happens next" retention;
- long-form YouTube expansion.

Weakness:
- rights complexity;
- privacy;
- graphic/ad-suitability risk;
- very different viewer expectation.

Separate brand later.

### B. Sports / card breaks / collectibles
Strength:
- repeated $2–$3 CPM campaign evidence;
- dramatic reaction footage;
- commerce/affiliate potential.

Separate brand if campaign supply remains durable.

### C. AI fictional found footage
Strength:
- fully owned visual IP;
- high automation;
- infinite inventory.

Weakness:
- unproven audience quality;
- AI-slop risk.

Separate fictional brand with clear AI/synthetic disclosure.

### D. Toys / unboxing / collectibles
Strength:
- recurring consumer campaigns;
- strong commerce intent.

Weakness:
- campaign briefs often demand a dedicated toy/unboxing identity and demographic history.

Only launch if at least several recurring campaigns justify maintaining the page.

### E. Finance / fintech
Strength:
- high commercial value;
- current clipping campaigns can pay solid CPMs.

Weakness:
- weak fit with MomentCircuit;
- higher factual/compliance burden;
- poor entertainment-audience overlap.

Do not mix into MomentCircuit.

### F. Tech / AI / software
Keep separate under LogicAndLayers / existing tech-product work.

Do not dilute either brand.

## Explicit exclusions

Never pursue on MomentCircuit:
- gambling/casino campaigns;
- deposit-match promos;
- crypto-pump material;
- stolen sports footage;
- raw TV/movie scene farms;
- gore/shock footage;
- politics/news as a generic traffic hack;
- random ecommerce ads;
- unrelated beauty/food/local-business promos;
- campaigns requiring false demographic/audience claims.

## Content mix for MomentCircuit

Initial measured hypothesis:

- **35% gaming moments**
- **30% streamer/creator moments**
- **20% creator/internet-culture lore & explainers**
- **10% celebrity/music/pop-culture**
- **5% licensed entertainment/podcast experiments**

Paid campaign posts are distributed inside these pillars rather than treated as a separate random category.

Do not force exact percentages if current high-quality source supply differs; coherence matters more than quota.

## Revenue hierarchy

For MomentCircuit, prioritize monetization in this order:

1. **paid clipping campaigns that fit the audience**;
2. **native platform monetization from original 60–120s story cuts**;
3. **creator/brand retainers or fixed-per-post deals**;
4. **sponsorships**;
5. **affiliate/commerce opportunities naturally related to gaming/creator culture**;
6. **long-form YouTube expansion** from proven short-form stories.

A higher CPM does not outrank a stronger long-term audience if the campaign damages account coherence.

## Account-value test

Before accepting a campaign, ask:

> If this campaign paid $0, could this post still plausibly make a MomentCircuit follower happy?

If no, reject it.

That single test prevents the account from becoming an ad dump.

## Future account portfolio

Only after MomentCircuit proves views + publishing + payout:

1. **MomentCircuit** — gaming / streamers / creator culture / pop culture.
2. **Incident brand** — rights-cleared bodycam / real-world stories.
3. **Sports/collectibles brand** — only if repeated sports-card campaign economics remain attractive.
4. **Synthetic found-footage brand** — only if Higgsfield pilots prove retention.

Do not launch all four at once.
